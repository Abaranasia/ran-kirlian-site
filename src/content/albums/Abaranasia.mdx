The music of Abaranasia was recorded between October and December 2003, 23 years ago now. These tracks remained stored and forgotten for a long time on a hard drive that I had considered lost for years. Fortunately, the drive was recovered not long ago, and inside I found not only these tracks, already arranged as an album, but also a wealth of other material in various stages of development.

The tracks that make up this album were recorded exclusively with my old Ensoniq Fizmo synthesizer. Over the past few months, they have been cleaned up, produced, and subtly enhanced using effects such as Valhalla Supermassive and Valhalla Shimmer. In my opinion, these additions have improved the original recordings while preserving their essence.

Rediscovering this music has been an emotional, immersive, and deeply revealing experience. It has opened a profound dialogue with the person I was at the beginning of my journey and has rekindled my passion for music, as well as my connection with the voice of one of my most beloved synthesizers, which has been part of my studio ever since.

In contrast with the artistic journey I have taken since then, these pieces, most of them improvised, evoke a world from the past—lost and forgotten for a very long time. A world that feels unreal and filled with fantasy, yet somehow tangible. It awakens in me a deep sense of nostalgia while, at the same time, fuelling a powerful flame of creativity and a passion for exploration. Abaranasia is that forgotten world, lost in time, where the treasures and remnants of an ancient culture still survive. A world that, after all these years, longs to be explored once again.
